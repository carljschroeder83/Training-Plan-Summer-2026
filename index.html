Programme aims — in priority order

1. Threshold velocity: 4:22 → 4:14/km

Rewritten 5 Sep after the Week 12 Parkrun. The original target was 4:08/km on the way to sub-19:30, which assumed VDOT 51. The race says VDOT 48 (20:50 official, ~20:25 wind-adjusted), corroborated by three separate sessions in Weeks 11 and 12. Threshold at 48 is 4:20, so the block runs 4:22 down to 4:14 and the benchmark target is 20:00-20:20. Everything else is supporting cast. Tuesday threshold never moves and never gets swapped, including on the road. Progress by adding time at pace, THEN dropping pace — never both in the same week.

2. Easy running volume: 45 → 55km/week

The 29km figure came from Week 7, which swapped its long run for a ride — Week 6 was already 45km, and that is the honest baseline. Threshold sits on an aerobic base, and the weeks as written now reach ~55km in Week 11. Volume gets added on Thursday and Sunday only — never to a quality day. The Wednesday ride exists to buy aerobic load without the bone and tendon cost of more running miles at 105kg; that is its job, not variety.

3. A VO2 stimulus that actually lands

Supporting work, not the headline. Week 7 showed the speed is already there — you ran 400m at 3:15/km. What's missing is the ability to hold sub-4:00, which is a threshold and durability limitation, not a VO2max ceiling. Constant-length reps of 3–5min, recovery capped at ~60% of rep duration, same pace every rep.

4. Body composition — a Weeks 8–12 project only

Worth stating plainly because it competes with everything above: running economy shifts by roughly 1.4s/km per kg, so 4–5kg is worth about 25–35 seconds over 5K — comparable to what eight weeks of threshold work will give you. But a deficit degrades session quality and recovery, so if you're going to do it, the window is Weeks 8–12, modest, protein held high, stopping at Week 13. No dieting through the sharpening phase. Sleep is upstream of all of it — at 6.4h you're suppressing training adaptation and appetite regulation at the same time.

5. Strength: maintenance from Week 9

No development phase. Loads hold rather than climb, split squats stay out, RDLs stay capped at 3×5, and strength lives on Tuesday PM after the run — which keeps heavy posterior chain work more than 72 hours from Saturday and stops it drifting into Thursday.

Session strategy — the one-variable rule

Every session has exactly one target variable

Everything else is a constraint you hold constant so the target variable means something. Most sessions go wrong because the athlete optimises a constraint — usually pace — and destroys the target. Open the 🎯 intent panel on any session card for its aim, target variable, execution rule and named failure mode.

The 30-second pre-session ritual

Before you leave the house, answer three questions out loud: 1. What is this session's target variable — what am I trying to accumulate? 2. What is the one number I must not exceed? 3. What does failure look like today? Naming the failure mode in advance is what stops you doing it in the first 60 seconds, when the legs are fresh and you feel good.

Race-day warm-up — the priming protocol

Time trial / Parkrun Countdown from the gun
  • T−45 to T−30 — 12–15min easy jog @ 6:00–6:20/km. Conversational. This is circulation, not fitness.
  • T−28 — 4× 20s strides, full walk-back recovery. Neuromuscular only; do not let these become efforts.
  • T−22 to T−19 — THE PRIMER: 2–3min continuous @ 4:12–4:16/km. Re-anchored to VDOT 48 — the number that matters is that it sits above lactate threshold, not the digits. It must be above lactate threshold — a stride is too short and an easy jog is too soft. You should finish it breathing hard and recover completely.
  • T−19 to T−6 — kit change, toilet, easy walking and light jogging. Do not sit down and do not stand still.
  • T−3 — 2× 15s accelerations to goal pace, then walk to the line.
  • T−0 to 200m — distance-based governor. Glance at the watch at 200m; if it reads faster than 4:08/km, ease off immediately. Do not pace the opener by feel.

Why the primer works. Starting cold, oxygen uptake lags demand for the first 90–120 seconds and the shortfall is covered anaerobically. That buffer feels free — no breathing signal, no leg heaviness — so you pace off a perception that has not yet caught up with the cost, and add 8–15s/km of stride length. A prior bout above threshold speeds VO2 kinetics, raises baseline motor unit recruitment and reduces the slow component, so the effort feels like what it costs from the first stride. The recovery window matters: less than ~6min and you carry residual fatigue to the line, more than ~15min and the effect washes out. Aim for 9–12min between the end of the primer and the gun. At 105kg the unprimed oxygen deficit is larger in absolute terms than for a lighter runner, so there is more here to gain than the textbook average.

Week 12: the evidence for all of this

20:50 official, 23rd of 419. The Parkrun that re-anchored the plan is also its clearest worked example. The warm-up ran 10:17 easy, then stopped dead for 4:36 while heart rate fell from 136 to 91, then strides, then finished 12:22 before the gun. Heart rate at the gun was 96. Corrected against true kilometres — GPS under-read the course by 1.6%, so the watch splits all read slow — the race was 3:54 / 4:14 / 4:19 / 4:15 / 4:08. The opener was 21s/km faster than the pace that followed it, run on a stride length of 1.42m against the 1.30m the race settled at; cadence varied by 0.8spm across the whole 5K, so the overshoot was entirely stride length, exactly as it is on the track and on the bike. The encouraging half: heart rate climbed cleanly and monotonically to 172, there was no fade, and the last kilometre was the fastest of the race — a negative-split finish out of a blown start. The engine held. The first 90 seconds, and the twelve standing minutes before them, are what cost the time.

The same problem shows up on Tuesday

Opener overshoot is not a discipline failure, it is an unprimed system reporting bad information — and it appears wherever the first hard effort follows a gap. Week 12's threshold session had 171 seconds between the end of the warm-up and the start of rep 1, which is long enough for HR and VO2 to decay most of the way back to baseline: rep 1 was effectively a second cold start. The plan already says prime before rep 1, strides then start within 90 seconds — treat that as a hard rule, not a suggestion. Applies to threshold, VO2 and every time trial.

Hybrid strength sessions — detail

Why heavy and low-volume, not light and high-rep

Runners do not lift for muscular endurance or size. The adaptation is neural and tendinous: more motor unit recruitment, higher rate of force development, and greater musculotendinous stiffness, so the leg spring returns more elastic energy per stride. Running economy improves because of that, not because the muscle got better at doing lots of reps. Force magnitude drives it, not accumulated work — reviews in trained distance runners (Beattie 2014, Blagrove 2018, Berryman 2018) consistently find heavy and plyometric protocols improve economy while submaximal high-repetition work does little or nothing. Tendon is the clearest case: Arampatzis showed high-strain loading raises tendon stiffness while low-strain loading at matched volume and duration does not, so equal-cumulative-load reasoning is the wrong currency. There is also a practical cost — 3×10-12 back squat at 65-70% is 30+ reps of heavy eccentric loading, which is exactly the posterior chain DOMS that already forced RDLs down to 3×5 and split squats out of VO2 weeks. And at 105kg, added muscle mass is a cost, not a benefit. The correct dose is heavy and brief: 3×5 at 80%, fifteen reps, Prilepin optimal. The original 3×3 at 82% was nine reps and fell below the effective range entirely — light enough to feel easy and short enough to train nothing. The exception is the hotel Session H at 3×6 unilateral: when the heaviest dumbbells are 32kg you cannot reach the force magnitude, so split squats raise per-limb force instead and the goal shifts from stimulus to retention. That is the best available option, not the better one.

Session A Power + lower body
  • Back squat — Ph1: 3×5 @70% (112kg) · Ph2: builds to 3×4 @80% (128kg) · Ph3: 3×3 @85% (136kg)
  • Romanian deadlift — 3×6 @80–100kg, progressing 5kg every 3 weeks. Cap at 3×5 in VO2 weeks — hamstring DOMS was showing up in run quality.
  • Push press — 3×5 @70–80kg. Keeps the overhead pattern alive.
  • Weighted pull-ups or lat pulldown — 3×6, add load or reps every 2 weeks
  • Bulgarian split squat — 2×8/leg, bodyweight → light DB → heavier DB across phases. Drop entirely in VO2 weeks (hip flexor soreness has been hitting the Saturday session).
  • Core — Hollow hold 3×40s, side plank 3×30s/side
Session B HIIT / metabolic circuit
  • Kettlebell swings — 4×15 → 4×20 → 5×15 across phases
  • Box jumps or broad jumps — 3×5. Plyometric power; direct running speed transfer.
  • DB goblet squat — 3×10, load increasing each phase
  • Push-up variation — 3×12 (standard → archer → weighted)
  • Renegade rows — 3×8/side.
  • Suitcase or farmer carry — 3×30m, load increasing each phase

Session B is retired from Week 10 — the Wednesday sweet spot ride takes its slot so Saturday VO2 running can go weekly. From Week 9, Session A becomes maintenance only: loads hold rather than climb, split squats stay out, RDLs capped at 3×5, and it runs Tuesday PM after the threshold session. Never Wednesday or Thursday — that puts heavy posterior chain work inside 48h of Saturday.

Travel rules

One threshold or quality run. Even 10min tempo in a 5km counts.

Priority 2 (keep if possible)

One easy run of 7–10km. Post-flight shakeouts count.

Drop first

Long run and strength sessions. Don't force on travel fatigue.

2-session minimum

Any week with 2 runs logged is maintenance, not a lost week.

Fatigue management

The soreness rule

If legs are sore on a quality run day, swap for easy. If sore on a strength day, drop to bodyweight only or skip.

Strength and running same day

Always run first. Quality run after heavy squats degrades badly; strength after easy run is fine.

VO2 stimulus comes from rep DURATION, not rep speed

Oxygen uptake takes roughly 90–120 seconds of hard running to reach VO2max — the kinetics are the rate limiter, not the effort. Run a rep faster and you don't arrive sooner, you just finish before the stimulus lands. At supramaximal pace, time at VO2max goes down. Keep reps ≥3 minutes, run them at the SLOW end of the band, and judge the session on minutes accumulated, not on rep splits.

Recoveries prime the next rep

Jog them — never stand or walk. The point isn't keeping HR high for its own sake: if VO2 only falls to ~70% between reps, rep 2 reaches VO2max in 40–50s instead of 120s, and rep 3 faster still. That priming compounds. Jog at 6:30–7:00/km, continuous. And prime before rep 1 — strides straight into the first rep, starting within 90 seconds. A long stand before the opener wastes your longest, most valuable rep.

Judge VO2 sessions on RELATIVE HR, not absolute BPM

No HRmax has been measured yet, so absolute BPM targets in this plan are estimates — don't chase them. But there is one test that works without knowing your max, because it compares two of your own sessions: a VO2 session should peak HIGHER than a threshold session in the same week. In Week 7 it did the opposite — Tuesday's threshold run peaked at 172, Saturday's track ladder at 165. Reps that finish before oxygen uptake arrives, separated by full recoveries, produce a lower cardiovascular peak than a sustained threshold block, however fast the splits look. That comparison is the diagnostic. Once the Week 8 hill test gives a real max, absolute targets become usable too.

Descending ladders are R sessions wearing a VO2 costume

A ladder shortens the rep exactly as HR is climbing, so the cardiovascular stimulus never lands — and because each rep is shorter than the last, the session feels progressively easier and finishes feeling great. Week 7's ladder ran 1200 at 3:57, then 3:50, 3:44, 3:32, and 400m at 3:15/km, with equal-time recoveries. Excellent neuromuscular work, near-zero VO2 stimulus. Ladders are retired as the Saturday VO2 vehicle. Keep them as an occasional fun session if you want — but log them as repetition work and don't count them toward weekly quality.

A written pace band is a hypothesis, not a fact

The threshold ladder in this plan was drawn in June off a projection. When reality disagrees twice, reality wins. Week 9 delivered 30min at 4:26 against a 4:20–4:24 band; Week 10 delivered 20min at 4:30 against 4:18–4:22. Same route, same 17–19°C, no fade inside either session — Week 10's final 323m was the fastest segment of its 15min block, with zero opener overshoot. That pattern is not a collapsing athlete; it is a band set 6–8s/km ahead of current fitness. The one-variable rule cuts both ways: you don't drop pace until the current pace is held with control, and writing the next rung down doesn't make the previous one yours. Two consecutive sessions below band = hold, don't progress. A clean 30min at 4:26 is worth more as a Parkrun predictor than a failed 36min at 4:18.

Long run ceiling — 100 minutes

The long run is capped at 100 minutes (~17km) for the rest of the plan, and it is a TIME target, not a distance one. Three reasons. One: for a 5K the limiters are threshold velocity and time at VO2max — neither is trained by minutes 100–120 of Z1 running. The glycogen-depletion and fat-oxidation adaptations that justify a 2hr run are marathon adaptations. Two: at 105kg the impact cost of those extra 20 minutes is roughly 2,500 additional footstrikes at 2.5–3× bodyweight, and it lands in Week 11 — the same week as peak threshold and peak VO2. Three: the standard guideline caps the long run at 25–30% of weekly running volume; 120min was 36% of Week 11. At 100min it is 31% and the week has one fewer peak in it. The fast finish stays — that is the part that transfers to km 4 of a 5K.

Deload weeks — cut quality, hold easy volume

Weeks 4, 8, 12 and 15 are planned deloads. The standard advice is to cut everything ~40%; that's the wrong prescription here, because running volume is already the thin part of the picture. Quality volume drops ~35%; easy running is held or slightly increased. Don't skip them — this is where adaptation happens.
